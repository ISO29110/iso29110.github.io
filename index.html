Win customer trust with a standard built for small companies.

ISO/IEC 29110 is the international standard for software and systems development in Very Small Entities: companies, departments or project teams of up to 25 people. It gives you a proven, lightweight way to deliver on time, reduce rework, and show clients that your process meets an international benchmark, without the cost and paperwork of standards designed for large enterprises.

00 · Introduction · Who develops the standard

Built by WG24, an international working group for very small entities

ISO/IEC 29110 is developed and maintained by Working Group 24 (WG24) of ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 7, the international committee for software and systems engineering standards. WG24 was formed specifically because existing standards were too large and complex for small companies to adopt. Its experts come from industry, national standards bodies and universities.

Its mission is to give very small entities a practical way to be recognised for producing quality software and systems. That means short, easy-to-use guides and profiles that scale, and that stay consistent with established standards such as ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207 and ISO 9001, with minimal disruption to how small companies already work.

How WG24 came about

2004

At the SC 7 meeting in Brisbane, Canada raises the need for standards suited to small organisations.

Mar 2005

TISI in Thailand hosts experts to advance the idea and define the target: organisations and projects of 1–25 people.

May 2005

The SC 7 plenary in Finland approves a ballot to develop lifecycle profiles and guidelines for VSEs.

Oct 2005

WG24 is formed, with 12 founding countries. Its first working sessions are held in Italy.

2006

India and Mexico join. Mexico's national standard NMX-059 is adopted as an input document.

2011 →

The Basic profile guide is published, followed by the Entry profile, systems engineering profiles and Deployment Packages.

01 · Business value

Why small companies adopt it

Most software is built by small firms, but most quality standards were written for large ones. ISO/IEC 29110 takes the practices that matter most and packages them at a scale a small team can actually run.

🤝

Win more contracts

Certification is independent evidence of a disciplined process. That helps in government tenders, with enterprise clients, and with overseas customers who ask "what's your quality process?"

📅

Deliver predictably

A written plan, regular progress tracking and formal change control mean fewer surprises on scope, schedule and budget.

🐞

Cut rework

Agreed requirements, reviews and planned testing catch defects early, when they are cheapest to fix.

🧩

Reduce key-person risk

Version control, traceability and maintenance documentation keep knowledge in the company, not just in one developer's head.

💰

Affordable to adopt

The management and engineering guides and Deployment Packages are freely available, and you can adopt practices one at a time.

📈

Room to grow

Four profiles let you start small and move up as your team and number of projects grow.

02 · Fit check

Is ISO/IEC 29110 right for your business?

A good fit if you…

  • Have up to 25 people in the company, department or project
  • Build software (or systems) for customers or for internal users
  • Want clients and partners to see evidence of a quality process
  • Have had projects slip on schedule, budget or scope
  • Rely heavily on a few key people's knowledge

Consider other standards if you…

  • Build safety-critical systems (medical, aviation, automotive); these need domain standards
  • Have well over 25 people in development, where ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207 or CMMI may fit better
  • Need a company-wide management system rather than a development process (e.g. ISO 9001)
  • Mainly need information security certification (ISO/IEC 27001)

04 · What you implement

The Basic profile in business terms

Project Management (PM) keeps the work on plan and the customer informed. Software Implementation (SI) turns requirements into a tested, delivered product. Everything starts from the customer's Statement of Work.

Customer's Statement of Work→ PM.1 Plan→ PM.2 Execute ⇄ PM.3 Control⇄ SI.1 – SI.6 Build→ PM.4 Close→ Accepted product
05 · Deployment Packages

Ready-made toolkits for putting the standard into practice

A Deployment Package (DP) is a free, self-contained set of artefacts that helps a small company implement one part of ISO/IEC 29110: process steps, templates, checklists, a worked example and tool suggestions. You don't have to adopt the whole standard at once. Implement one package, see the benefit, then add the next.

What's inside every package

All DPs follow the same 12-part structure, so once your team has used one, the rest feel familiar. The highlighted parts are the ones staff use day-to-day.

TIP

Section 3 maps the package to the exact ISO/IEC 29110 tasks it covers, and Section 10 to other standards such as ISO 9001, ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207 and CMMI. That's useful evidence when preparing for an audit.

06 · Adoption roadmap

From first step to certificate

A typical path for a small company. Timelines vary, but working one Deployment Package at a time keeps the effort manageable alongside client work.

  1. 1

    Get commitment and a champion

    Management agrees on the business goal (e.g. win tenders, reduce overruns) and names one person to lead the improvement effort.

  2. 2

    Assess where you are

    Compare current practices with the Basic profile to find the gaps that matter most to the business.

    DP Self-Assessment

  3. 3

    Pick a pilot project

    Choose a real but low-risk project to trial the new practices before rolling them out company-wide.

    DP Select and Conduct Pilot Projects

  4. 4

    Deploy packages incrementally

    Start with the foundations (Project Management, Version Control, Requirements Analysis), then add design, construction, testing, verification and delivery practices.

    DP One package at a time, guided by the gap analysis

  5. 5

    Use it on real projects and keep evidence

    Run at least one full project with the new process. Keep the plans, records and reports: these are what an auditor will look at.

  6. 6

    Internal review, then certification

    Run an internal check against the profile, fix gaps, then engage an accredited certification body for the audit.

07 · Certification

Getting independently recognised

Part 3 of the standard defines how VSEs are assessed, audited and certified. Certification is done by an accredited certification body against a specific profile, usually Entry or Basic for a first certificate.

08 · Roles

You don't need to hire seven people

The standard defines responsibilities, not headcount. In a small company one person typically covers several roles. The only rule of thumb is that work should be checked by someone other than its author.

09 · Key documents

The records your process produces

These work products are also the evidence an auditor looks for. Most can be lightweight: a page, a spreadsheet, a ticket or a wiki entry is often enough.

10 · FAQ

Common questions from business owners

Does it work with Agile or Scrum?

Yes. ISO/IEC 29110 says what must be done and produced, not which method to use. Sprint planning, backlogs, reviews and retrospectives can provide the required activities and records, and guidance exists for agile contexts.

How much documentation will it add?

Less than most people expect. The Basic profile asks for a focused set of work products, and many can live in tools you already use: an issue tracker, a Git repository, a shared drive or a wiki.

How is it different from ISO 9001 or CMMI?

ISO 9001 is a general quality management system for any organisation; CMMI is a maturity model aimed mainly at larger organisations. ISO/IEC 29110 is specific to software and systems development and is sized for teams of up to 25. The Deployment Packages include mappings to ISO 9001, ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207 and CMMI if you plan to pursue those later.

Do we need a consultant?

Not necessarily. The guides and Deployment Packages are designed so a small company can implement them itself. Some firms use a coach for the gap assessment or a pre-audit review.

Where should we start?

Run the Self-Assessment package, choose a pilot project, then deploy Project Management and Version Control first. They give the quickest visible benefit.
