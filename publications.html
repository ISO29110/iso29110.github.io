Featured: Two articles in the IEEE Computer tell the story of ISO/IEC 29110: what WG24 set out to do, and what users reported once the standard was in use.

IEEE Computer · August 2016

Systems and Software Engineering Standards for Very Small Entities: Accomplishments and Overview ↗

C. Y. Laporte (ÉTS), R. V. O'Connor (Dublin City University)

28countries reached by WG24's first survey, in 9 languages
1,300+official comments processed over five review cycles
10–18%rework in first ISO/IEC 29110 projects, vs. 40–50% typical
  • In 2005, the Thailand Industrial Standards Institute and Thailand's software promotion agency sponsored the working group that kick-started the standards. ISO and IEC then mandated WG24 to develop them.
  • Surveyed VSEs asked for lightweight, easy-to-understand standards with examples, templates and checklists, and for recognition or certification.
  • Deployment Packages and translations helped adoption. In Thailand, more than 10 universities teach ISO/IEC 29110.
  • Brazil, Mexico, Peru, Japan, Thailand and Uruguay adopted it as a national standard.
IEEE Computer · August 2020

Delivering Software- and Systems-Engineering Standards for Small Teams ↗

C. Y. Laporte (ÉTS), J. Mejía Miranda (CIMAT)

88%of VSEs completely or largely satisfied with their implementation
~80%of their customers completely or largely satisfied
450+organizations certified in Thailand
  • A 2018 survey in 29 countries gathered feedback from VSEs, their customers, auditors and educators.
  • Within six months of implementation, VSEs saw gains in processes (73%), productivity (64%) and quality (58%).
  • The series is taught in 20+ countries, and more than 200 papers have been written about it.
  • In Mexico, 10+ universities certified their student software development centres.
